How it works

  1. Track your face. MediaPipe finds 478 points on your face, 30 times a second, on your phone's GPU.
  2. Watch your skin. The average color of your forehead and cheeks is recorded every frame.
  3. Pull out the pulse. POS cancels lighting and motion changes and keeps the tiny color change caused by blood flow.
  4. Find the rhythm. A band-pass filter keeps heart-speed changes, and an FFT finds the strongest rhythm: your heart rate.
Good to know
  • Works best in even light, holding still. Accuracy is typically within a few BPM when still.
  • Darker skin reflects less of the pulse signal; good light helps a lot. POS and CHROM handle it better than the green channel.
  • Breathing and HRV are experimental. This is not a medical device.

Methods: Wang et al. 2017 (POS), de Haan & Jeanne 2013 (CHROM), Verkruysse et al. 2008 (green), Wu et al. 2012 (Eulerian Video Magnification). Face tracking: Google MediaPipe.

Each dot pairs one beat interval with the next. A wide, round cloud means your heart rhythm varies from beat to beat, which usually happens when you're relaxed. A tight, thin cloud means a steadier rhythm, common when stressed or exercising. Camera timing is limited to 30 frames per second, so treat these numbers as a rough guide.
